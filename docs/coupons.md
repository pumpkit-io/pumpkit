# Coupon implementation and operations contract

This is the implementation contract for spec #392, settled by #396. Privileged issuance, preview, cardless and subscriber redemption, future paid signup and deferred monthly/annual changes are implemented behind the shared setting. The public English and Italian documents describe the complete feature; publishing them does not activate it or constitute legal approval. Self-hosters supply their own legal documents.

## Activation and provider evidence

`COUPONS_ENABLED=false` is the one default-off feature setting. Off means no issuance, preview, redemption or coupon-specific paid signup/plan-change actions and no coupon affordances. Ordinary billing and instances that sell no plans keep their existing behavior. Coupon functionality also requires configured plans. Enable it only in isolated test environments until final #394 passes.

Implement #396, #395, #397–#405 in order with normal checks, fake-gateway tests, adapter contracts and PR review. Real Stripe validation runs only after their PRs merge. It is the activation gate, not an implementation prerequisite. Reuse `pro-coupons-t394`'s `backend/tests/billing/test_stripe_deferral_live.py` and `docs/stripe-coupon-verification.md`. Their partial evidence is historical and does not validate the merged application. Hosted Checkout through its first paid period and authenticated notice/configuration inspection remain outstanding.

The recorded experiment accepted an absolute `trial_end` of provider time plus 730 × 86400 seconds and rejected one second later, in classic and flexible modes. Include remaining paid time in this bound and apply it to cardless claims too. Preserve original paid/trial coverage and allowance anchors locally. A Stripe `trialing` deferral is not an introductory Pumpkit trial. Existing `end_trial` charges immediately and must not consume free time. The recorded portal approach uses no proration and `trial_update_behavior=continue_trial`; respect the effective `cancel_at`, even when `cancel_at_period_end` is false. These are implementation inputs pending final verification, not permission to reduce benefits if a provider case fails.

## Acceptance contract

Terms version `2026-11-09` is shared by both languages and the backend. The Privacy Policy has the same update date, including the November 7 site-statistics disclosures. The existing paid subscription and top-up flows retain their three separate boxes and acceptance evidence. Every later paid signup requires new paid acceptance, including a future subscription arranged during free access. Having a saved card or coupon acceptance is insufficient.

Coupon confirmation uses two unticked boxes. There is no paid immediate-start/withdrawal waiver and no new automatic-renewal acceptance. Clause numbers and anchors match the public documents:

1. English: “I accept the Terms and have read the Privacy Policy.” Italian: “Accetto i Termini e ho letto l’Informativa sulla privacy.” Link each document in the selected language.
2. English: “Under arts. 1341 and 1342 of the Italian Civil Code, I specifically approve clauses 9 (Suspension and termination by the Operator), 16 (Limitation of liability), 19 (Changes to these Terms) and 21 (Competent court).” Italian: “Ai sensi degli artt. 1341 e 1342 del Codice civile, approvo specificamente le clausole 9 (Sospensione e risoluzione da parte del Gestore), 16 (Limitazione di responsabilità), 19 (Modifiche ai presenti Termini) e 21 (Foro competente).” Link each clause. Do not include clause 12's automatic renewal or clause 14's paid refunds in this approval.

Before these boxes show the exact start/end, full monthly allowance and next agreed payment, or “No automatic payment” / “Nessun pagamento automatico”. For a cardless recipient show “Free access ends on {date}. This creates no subscription or obligation to pay.” / “L’accesso gratuito termina il {date}. Non crea un abbonamento né un obbligo di pagamento.” A subscriber instead sees the preserved paid coverage and actual deferred payment under their existing renewal choice.

#398 records authenticated user, UTC acceptance timestamp, Terms version, language, both affirmative boxes, accepted preview/benefit dates, payment consequences and operation identity, associated with that redemption. `requested_immediate_start` is false if the existing acceptance model is reused. A coupon-specific request must not require it to be true. Preview binds the displayed version and exact offer; confirmation revalidates both. Missing/false acceptance is refused without a claim or provider mutation; an old version or changed offer returns a conflict requiring a fresh preview and cleared boxes. Duplicate confirmation recovers its original result and evidence, never another benefit. An instance without operator details follows the existing no-Terms contract rather than fabricating acceptance.

#397 delivers preview and the shared form; #398 implements local redemption evidence; #401 extends the same evidence to durable provider operations. No sign-in acceptance or new MCP redemption tool is added.

## Identity and key custody

#395 stores recipient assignments as keyed fingerprints, with no plaintext recipient email. Normalize exactly as account creation does (`email.strip().lower()` after email validation); do not remove dots or plus suffixes. Recipient matching uses the current confirmed email supplied by X. Claims retain both that normalized email and the stable X account as separate keyed fingerprints: either match refuses another claim, including after a contact change or account deletion. Distinct X accounts sharing a claimed email cannot claim that coupon again. Different coupons still stack. Keep the existing email `claim` purpose; X account claims use `claim-x-account`, separate from recipient matching and the Trial key ring. Include a purpose separator and coupon identifier in HMAC-SHA256 inputs so fingerprints for unrelated coupons cannot be linked without the key. Store a key identifier with every fingerprint. Code digests are separate from email fingerprints; raw codes appear once in intentional CLI output, never in logs, URLs or analytics.

`COUPON_IDENTITY_KEYS` is a secret JSON key ring: `{"active":"v1","keys":{"v1":"<base64 of 32 random bytes>"}}`. #395 validates it before enabling issuance. The active key writes new fingerprints. Matching evaluates every retained key so a new active key cannot reset an old claim. Retired keys remain available for matching until no retained assignment, claim or unresolved operation needs them, and their last containing backup has expired. Do not try to rehash old fingerprints without the verified email. An absent referenced key fails closed for affected operations and alerts the person running the instance; it never treats the identity as unclaimed.

Keep the key ring in the instance secret store, restricted to its application and privileged tooling. Back it up encrypted separately from the database, with access restricted to the same trusted people. Restore the database and its corresponding complete key ring together. Test recovery before rotation. Rotate by adding a new unique key identifier and making it active, retaining old keys for reads; never replace a key's bytes in place. A no-deadline coupon can require its old key indefinitely. To retire a compromised key, permanently disable affected unclaimable/claimable codes as appropriate, resolve all operations and preserve existing benefits, then wait out record and backup retention before removing it. Key loss is not repaired by resetting claim eligibility; restore it or keep affected operations disabled.

## Retention and purge

These periods implement purpose limitation rather than applying the tax retention period to all coupon data. Fingerprints remain pseudonymous personal data. The choices implement the product's same-email and same-X-account claim promise while limiting ended-benefit and recovery history. They are product retention decisions, not a claim of legal approval. The [GDPR's storage-limitation and lawful-processing rules](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679) inform the contract.

A coupon becomes permanently unclaimable at its UTC deadline, permanent disable, or exhaustion by completed claims with no pending reservation that can restore capacity. Store this terminal time; disabling is irreversible. Pending operations still need reconciliation. Turning the feature off is temporary and does not terminate redeemability for retention purposes.

| Record                                              | Retain until                                                | Purge behavior and owner                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unclaimed recipient assignment                      | Permanently unclaimable and all related operations resolved | Remove fingerprint/key identifier within 30 days; #395. No-deadline codes retain their assignment while claimable.                                                        |
| Claim fingerprint, including after account deletion | Same terminal condition                                     | Remove within 30 days; #398. Keep a nonpersonal completed-claim count so purging cannot restore capacity.                                                                 |
| Active/queued benefit and its acceptance            | Benefit end and resolution of related operations            | Remove within 90 days of the later event; #398. Account deletion ends benefits and removes the account link immediately, retaining only required claim/recovery evidence. |
| Pending recovery intent/evidence                    | Confirmed reconciliation or definite refusal                | Never age out unresolved work; #401. Keep only stable operation/provider identifiers, accepted offer, target and original coverage facts needed for recovery.             |
| Resolved recovery/audit evidence                    | Resolution and end of related benefits                      | Remove within 90 days of the later event; #401. Do not keep raw provider payloads or recipient email.                                                                     |
| Backups containing removed data or old keys         | Last live removal                                           | Existing rotation removes copies within 30 days. Reapply purge after restore before serving traffic.                                                                      |

Where one row serves several purposes, remove unnecessary fields independently and retain only fields whose purpose remains. Account deletion detaches the user but preserves claim fingerprints and the minimum unresolved billing evidence. It must serialize with redemption/recovery and must not allow later recovery to grant orphaned access or charge a deleted account. A pending operation keeps enough deletion intent to finish safely; it does not retain the deleted profile or content. A legal hold must be separately justified, documented and limited to its actual records, not silently applied to every coupon.

#395 supplies privileged `uv run python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons purge` from `backend/`; #398 and #401 extend that command for their records. Run it daily with the same instance configuration and database privileges, including when `COUPONS_ENABLED=false`. It must be idempotent and safe against concurrent claims, disable and recovery, with the same lock order as redemption. Inspect its nonsecret counts and failures; retry a failed daily run immediately. Check unresolved operations daily, flag anything pending more than 24 hours and investigate until resolved; there is no destructive age-based timeout. #401 supplies the independent recovery invocation and its lock order. Do not log keys, codes, email assignments, fingerprints or full provider responses in purge/recovery output.

## Privileged coupon commands

The commands run with the instance's database and application configuration; they have no public endpoint. Access to the source alone does not allow issuance. Run migrations before using them. Each code is 32 cryptographically random bytes encoded for copying. The database stores its SHA-256 digest, never a recoverable code; recipient assignments use the keyed fingerprints above. Issuance metadata and benefit months cannot be changed through this tool.

Generate each identity key with `openssl rand -base64 32` in a trusted terminal and insert it in the protected JSON ring. Keep the output out of shared logs. Coupons must stay off outside isolated tests until final validation.

On a compose instance, use its configured backend:

```sh
docker compose exec backend python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons create --months 3 --recipient recipient@example.org --max-redemptions 1 --claim-deadline 2027-12-01T00:00:00Z
docker compose exec backend python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons list
docker compose exec backend python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons inspect coupon_ID
docker compose exec backend python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons disable coupon_ID
docker compose exec backend python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons purge
```

For a managed database outside compose, export the same protected instance settings, then run `uv run python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons ...` from `backend/`. Alternatively `uv run --env-file ../.env python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons ...` explicitly loads the root configuration. Do not point a development command at production accidentally.

Create requires positive whole `--months`; `--max-redemptions` defaults to one. Omit `--recipient` for a general code and `--claim-deadline` for no deadline. A deadline must have a UTC offset (`Z` or `+00:00`) and be strictly in the future. Recipient normalization matches sign-in: trim whitespace and lowercase the validated email, retaining dots and plus suffixes. Unsupported future horizons are refused later at preview/confirmation rather than silently shortening benefits.

Successful create prints one JSON object with `id` and the raw `code`. Deliver that code privately once. Avoid terminal recording, CI logs, redirected shared files and analytics. Losing it requires a new code; inspect/list cannot recover it. All other commands use the nonsecret `id` and never print the code, assigned email or fingerprint. Nonzero exit status means failure, with a sanitized explanation on stderr; a failed transaction prints no code.

Disable is permanent and idempotent. It stops new claims and leaves existing granted benefits intact. Inspect, list, disable and purge remain available while the feature is off so Operators can maintain existing records. Purge removes recipient fingerprints and their key identifiers at 30 days after permanent disable or deadline, preserving the restricted-recipient marker and immutable metadata. A still-claimable code without a deadline retains its assignment. The claim/recovery tickets extend purge for their additional records and pending-operation safety.

Run purge daily using the same protected settings, including while coupons are off. A cron/systemd job can invoke `docker compose exec -T backend python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons purge` from the instance directory. Check its nonsecret count and exit status; retry failures immediately. Current management locks coupon rows in identifier order, with purge skipping busy rows for the next run. Recipient redemption/recovery follows user, then coupon, then operation lock order; disable and purge never acquire a user lock after a coupon lock.

**Upgrade notes:** The migration adds only the coupon table. Existing instances need no key while the default setting stays off. Before isolated testing or eventual activation, configure and securely back up the key ring and install the daily purge job. Follow final #394 before enabling outside isolated tests. Already-issued codes require their matching database and complete key ring through upgrades and restores.

## Recipient preview

With coupons enabled in an isolated test instance, the Paywall offers **Have a coupon?** before paid checkout, including when a payment is on hold. **Check code** sends the code in an authenticated request body to `POST /api/v1/billing/coupons/preview`; it never puts it in a URL or analytics. Checking a code previews the offer without consuming it; the separate confirmation grants access.

Preview requires an available code, its verified recipient where restricted, compatible configured offers and a supported billing state. It checks current provider subscriptions and all customer invoices, so an older outstanding invoice cannot be hidden by a newer paid one. Pending changes, unsettled invoices and unsupported existing-subscription paths are nonconsuming refusals. No customer, subscription, claim, capacity reservation or access is created. Provider failures retain the code in the form for retry.

The response supplies UTC start/end timestamps, whole calendar months, the full monthly allowance, payment consequences, the applicable Terms version and a signed `revision`. Benefits beyond 730 × 86400 seconds are refused before calendar overflow is possible. Month calculation uses the original anchor rather than adding clipped month ends successively.

The revision uses the existing instance JWT signing secret with a separate `coupon-preview` salt. It authenticates the caller and coupon identifiers, displayed offer and preview instant, plus a digest of the issuance/recipient state. It contains no raw code, recipient email or fingerprint. Its payload expires ten minutes after preview. #398 must verify signature, expiry, caller and coupon identity, then recheck current availability, recipient, billing eligibility, offer/Terms and horizon before confirming; a changed offer requires a fresh preview and acceptance. Merely advancing the clock does not corrupt the authenticated original preview instant. A preview reserves neither its dates nor capacity. Rotating the JWT secret invalidates outstanding previews, which can safely be checked again.

## Cardless confirmation and access

Cardless redemption accepts a recipient with no running subscription or pending paid Checkout, including someone with remaining free access. Renewing monthly and annual subscribers and users finishing an existing introductory trial use the durable provider-backed flow below. Already-cancelled renewal preserves cancellation and uses the local-coverage path below. A former subscriber with a settled account can redeem without using their saved card. Confirmation reads provider eligibility under the user billing lock and creates no Stripe customer, subscription, Checkout or payment. An open paid Checkout must finish, expire or be cancelled by support before redemption; checking or refusing a coupon does not cancel it.

For an immediate cardless offer, the Paywall says **Starts when you redeem** and displays UTC calendar dates. Preview timestamps describe those dates; their seconds are not an already-running grant. The signed revision binds the months, displayed UTC dates, full monthly allowance, payment consequences, Terms and issuance state. Confirmation anchors the whole calendar-month interval at its current server time, after lock waits and provider checks. It requires a fresh preview if either displayed UTC date changed, or if any other offer term changed. This preserves full usable months without treating every elapsed second as a changed offer. The final result contains precise UTC timestamps. A January 31 anchor resets on February's last day and then March 31, calculated from the original anchor.

`POST /api/v1/billing/coupons/redemptions` accepts a caller-generated operation identifier, signed revision, language and the two cardless acceptance boxes above. It accepts no client dates, email or month quantity. The signed-in caller can recover the committed result with `GET /api/v1/billing/coupons/redemptions/{operation_id}`. Repeating the same operation and revision returns its original dates, including after disable, preview expiry or temporary feature deactivation. Reusing an identifier for another revision is refused. Keep the same identifier after a lost response. Result lookup and acceptance evidence remain available until the benefit's 90-day post-end purge; they are removed immediately on account deletion.

The user row is locked and freshly rechecked for existence and suspension, followed by the coupon row, then its claim and operation records. Disable locks only the coupon. Under that lock, identity matching checks every retained claim key before inserting an active-key fingerprint. Claims have a database uniqueness constraint, each benefit has one unique claim and each caller has unique operation identifiers. Benefit, accepted offer, count and result commit together. Exhaustion records a permanent terminal time. The local-only path creates no pending provider operations; the monthly path below extends deletion and purge for durable reservations.

Free access remains effective when coupons are temporarily disabled. Billing status, Onboarding, browser and connected app access and the publisher use the same half-open interval: access ends at the precise end timestamp. Monthly allowances use the accepted full caps, including existing top-ups under their ordinary spending rules. Scheduling projects from the original anchor and refuses publication at or after the known end. Ordinary checkout cannot replace remaining free access; use the explicit future paid-signup flow below. Current free access permits normal top-up purchases. A minimal account-bound history marker removes introductory trial eligibility after detailed benefit evidence is purged.

Purge now reports `records_purged`, counting recipient assignments, claim fingerprints and ended benefits removed. It removes claims 30 days after the first terminal time while keeping the completed count. It removes account-bound benefit, acceptance and replay detail 90 days after the end. Both remain independent: a terminal code's fingerprints may be removed while its already granted benefit continues.

### Support-requested account deletion

Use the privileged owning command after verifying the deletion request under the instance's support process:

```sh
docker compose exec backend python -m pumpkit.account_deletion --user-id user_ID
```

The command first locks the user, then its scheduled post rows in identifier order, then affected coupons. It refuses deletion while any post is publishing, so an X call already in flight can settle and retain its returned X identifier. Wait for publishing or recovery to finish before retrying; do not delete those rows manually. Locking waiting scheduled posts also prevents the publisher from claiming one between the check and account deletion. It refuses unresolved subscriptions, paid checkouts or invoice/payment obligations; a saved card alone is not an obligation. Resolve actual billing obligations before retrying. It does not cancel a subscription. It deletes the profile, sign-in links for its normalized email, sessions, content and account-bound benefit/acceptance/results through their owning services and database cascades. Only protected claims without the old account identifier and nonpersonal completed counts survive as specified above. Recreating the same email therefore cannot claim the same still-redeemable coupon again.

Stored Media files need a separate retryable cleanup after the database commit. The deletion transaction queues their storage keys without retaining a profile or email. File cleanup removes each queued key and then its queue record. On failure the command returns nonzero; retry it or run `python -m pumpkit.account_deletion --retry-files` with the same protected configuration. Run `--retry-files` daily alongside coupon purge, inspect failure status and retry immediately. Do not declare content deletion complete while queued files remain. Both commands work with coupons disabled.

### Appending free months in Settings

With coupons enabled, the plan card in Settings offers **Redeem coupon**, using the same preview and two-box confirmation as the Paywall. A distinct coupon appends after remaining free access. Preview shows the new grant's dates; the plan card shows the combined end prominently plus the current grant and queued ranges, and Usage retains the current period, usage and top-ups. After success those reads and access state refresh. Another browser reads the same committed coverage on reload. No subscription or automatic payment is created.

Each grant retains its own claim, acceptance, operation result and accepted monthly caps. Current-period caps and usage remain unchanged when a new offer has different caps; those caps apply only in its queued months. The grant persists the original UTC calendar anchor and its whole-month offset, so January 31 clips to February's last day and then returns to March 31. Purging an earlier ended grant never changes a queued grant's anchor. Purge still removes each grant's detail 90 days after its own end.

The signed preview binds the preceding grant and offset as well as the offer. Confirmation serializes on the user lock, so a competing append changes the offer and requires a new preview and acceptance. It never silently shifts the grant or consumes a stale claim. The combined end, including all remaining free time, must be at most 730 × 86400 seconds after confirmation; an unsupported offer is refused without consuming it. Deadlines govern confirmation only, and disabling a code preserves already queued months.

### Top-ups during free access

While free access is current, Usage offers the normal Buy top-up action, including for a recipient with no subscription or a provider subscription reported as trialing after deferral. The one-time purchase uses the existing pack, price and paid Terms confirmation; it creates no recurring subscription and preserves the full free period. Operators need no additional setting or product.

The full monthly allowance is spent first, then unexpired previously purchased packs, earliest expiry first. Packs retain their existing 12-month expiry and refund/dispute removal rules. Queued free months alone do not qualify a current introductory trial or overdue payment for purchase: their existing Subscribe now or payment action remains. Instances without plans or a configured top-up price offer no purchase. Already granted access and its top-ups continue when coupon issuance/redemption is disabled.

Support account deletion refuses while any Checkout is open or a completed one-time payment is unresolved, including asynchronous payments without an invoice. It does not expire or cancel payments automatically. Resolve an open Checkout by completing or explicitly expiring it through the provider; for a completed asynchronous payment, wait for settlement or obtain a confirmed canceled PaymentIntent through the provider where cancellation is supported. Paid, no-payment-required, expired and confirmed canceled one-time payment history does not block deletion. Missing or unknown provider facts require investigation before deletion. Top-up creation and deletion share the user lock, including after first-customer creation commits.

### Existing subscribers and durable confirmation

A paid-up renewing monthly or annual subscriber can redeem in Settings. Preview proves the current paid period from a positive settled invoice covering the single configured recurring item, and checks all invoices and pending Checkouts. Missing settlement facts, paused states and incompatible changes remain nonconsuming refusals. Cancelled renewal follows the local-coverage path described below. An existing introductory trial must have its original start/end and matching current item period, no previous coupon history and no deferral marker; its zero-dollar invoice alone never establishes paid coverage.

Redemption saves original paid coverage, monthly anchor and full allowance before changing Stripe. Stripe's copy stays truthful even when its deferral reports `trialing`; this is not a new introductory trial. Paid usage retains its original period and accepted caps. Free access begins at the actual paid-through date, with full monthly caps for each accepted grant. Its calendar calculation anchor is separate from its coverage start, so January 31 paid time can lead to February 29 free start and March 31 reset. The plan card shows paid-through, queued/current free dates and the actual deferred renewal. Subscribe now and the generic portal cannot charge away these months, including when coupons are off. **Cancel renewal** removes future renewal at the final free end and preserves paid/free coverage; retry after a provider error reads current cancellation first.

Before the first provider mutation, a committed operation records the accepted offer and Terms, reservation, customer/subscription/item/price identity, original coverage and absolute target. The provider request uses integer absolute `trial_end`, no proration and one stable operation idempotency key. A pending result is **Confirming redemption**, not success or a final claim. Capacity includes unresolved reservations; definite provider refusals release them. Disable stops new intents, while an already accepted intent remains recoverable. Pending operations survive preview/Terms expiry and the feature flag being turned off. Never create a new operation merely because the first response was lost.

Authenticated operation GET/retry and privileged recovery use the same reconciliation. Both read current provider truth before retrying: an attributed matching target completes locally; unchanged compatible original state may receive the same absolute update. Higher dates, identity changes, cancellation, ended state, passed targets and other conflicts remain reserved for investigation rather than being overwritten or extended relative to the retry time. After the target has passed, retained matching provider trial/operation evidence can still prove the applied deferral. If the provider has removed that evidence, recovery remains pending; support must inspect provider history. Stripe idempotency retention is not treated as a permanent ledger. Webhooks only reconcile confirmed provider changes and never commit inside their handlers.

The lock order is user, coupon identifiers in order, then operation/claim records. Checkout, top-up creation, Subscribe now, portal, cancellation and account deletion recheck pending operations under the user lock, including after early commits. Provider mutation holds that lock. Disable/purge take only coupon locks and never acquire user afterward. Deletion refuses unresolved operations even if the provider subscription has ended: reconcile billing before retrying deletion. Direct SQL deletion bypasses these guarantees and is unsupported.

Run the production recovery owner at least daily with the instance's protected settings, even while coupons are off:

```sh
docker compose exec -T backend python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons recover --list --limit 100
docker compose exec -T backend python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons recover --limit 100
docker compose exec -T backend python -m pumpkit.billing.coupons recover --operation-id coupon_operation_ID
```

Listing prints opaque recovery identifiers, status, creation time, attempt counts and sanitized recovery detail, never codes, fingerprints, email or provider responses. `--operation-id` takes the opaque `id` from that listing. Recovery uses bounded per-operation transactions; one conflict does not roll back other results. A pending/conflicted result exits nonzero, and an unresolved operation has no destructive timeout. Alert and investigate any operation older than 24 hours, retry transient failures promptly and inspect provider history for conflicts. Keep these operational reports private because they remain linkable account evidence. HTTP operation lookup exposes only the signed-in caller's operation; billing reads include that caller's pending identifier for recovery on another browser.

Purge never removes pending operation evidence, reserved claims or recipient keys needed by unresolved work. It also clears saved paid start/end, anchor and allowance fields from a retained subscription 90 days after the later of paid coverage end, related granted benefit end or operation resolution. Unresolved operations and current/queued benefits retain the fields. This cleanup locks users first and skips busy accounts before taking coupon locks, so concurrent redemption or recovery cannot lose a newly saved paid period. Provider-copy fields and current Usage remain unchanged; later eligible claims save their own current paid coverage. Known provider cancellation dates close the subscription access source independently of these disposable fields, even when a delayed webhook leaves its status active or trialing. Already earned paid and free coverage retain their own dates; expired coverage cannot regain access, buy top-ups or trigger Subscribe now after purge. Resolved operation detail expires 90 days after the later of resolution or accepted benefit end; a definite refusal with no granted benefit expires 90 days after refusal. Ordinary claim prevention retains its separate 30-day terminal-code rule. Completed account deletion removes resolved account-linked detail while keeping the existing minimal retained claim identity.

### Settling scheduled-post Top-ups after access ends

Publication access checks continue to use current access and its precise cancellation/free end. Settling an existing reservation instead reads the historical subscription or retained grant for its publish time, including terminal subscriptions and older grant chains. This accounting read cannot authorize writing or publishing. A provider's coupon deferral is never treated as a reduced introductory trial when its original local allowance evidence has been purged.

When history is no longer available, the transaction that first marks a scheduled post failed releases its stored reservations for unaccepted parts. It retains charges for accepted parts, including the existing protection for removed top-ups, and leaves other waiting reservations intact. Replayed publisher passes and deleting an already failed partial thread do not repeat that settlement. Deleting a still-waiting partial thread settles it once before retaining its live-part charges. No additional history is retained and no migration or new job is required.

### Annual coverage and existing introductory Trials

Annual subscribers retain every remaining paid day and the original monthly allowance resets. Three free months after a December 1 annual renewal move the next payment to March 1; the provider then starts a full annual interval from that new anchor. The combined remaining paid and free time must fit the same 730-day horizon. Repeated claims reuse the original calendar anchor, including leap-day clipping.

An introductory trial already underway finishes with its original start, end, usage and saved reduced caps. Full monthly free allowances follow it; the first payment moves to the final free end. The original trial end anchors free months, so January 31 leads to February 29 and then March 31. Receiving a benefit prevents a new introductory trial while preserving this one. Settings distinguishes the original introductory trial end, queued/current free dates and next payment. Top-ups stay unavailable during the original trial and become available during current free months. Subscribe now cannot charge away queued benefits.

Both cases use the same committed operation, absolute provider target, reconciliation, cancellation and purge as monthly claims. Provider trial fields remain truthful and never redefine the original reduced allowance. The nullable `preserved_trial` field distinguishes the kind of saved coverage in the existing `paid_start`, `paid_end`, `paid_anchor` and `paid_allowance` fields; null on older rows means paid coverage. The latest grant’s completed operation identifies its source subscription, including after earlier grant detail is purged. Retained snapshots from unrelated older subscriptions cannot block a new claim or bind cardless free access to provider history. Their normal retention and purge rules stay unchanged. Payment debt, processing invoices, pending Checkouts, incompatible changes and the combined 730-day horizon remain nonconsuming refusals.

Cancellation between preview and confirmation changes the offer and requires a fresh preview showing no payment. Settings distinguishes paid-through, queued/current free access and its final end from actual provider status. ## Arrange paid renewal during free access

With coupons enabled, Settings lets a recipient with current free access and no running subscription review a configured monthly or annual plan. The review shows the current price, interval and exact first-payment timestamp in UTC. A fresh paid acceptance agrees to that plan and date. A saved card, an old customer record and acceptance of a coupon never authorize this signup. No introductory trial is added. Each grant keeps its accepted monthly allowance, usage and calendar anchor.

Pumpkit commits the accepted offer and paid consent before creating a customer or Checkout. Hosted Checkout collects a card for future off-session payments, the full billing address and tax ID; it takes no payment. After reading the current completed SetupIntent and its customer-owned payment method, Pumpkit creates the subscription with automatic tax and the unchanged absolute free end. This uses the installed Stripe API's direct subscription `trial_end`, avoiding subscription Checkout's 48-hour minimum. Setup mode itself does not calculate automatic tax. The first full paid month or year starts at the agreed end. The provider's `trialing` status describes this deferral; Settings continues to describe free access.

An open or uncertain signup is shown as being confirmed, with Resume checkout, Check status and Cancel signup actions. It blocks conflicting billing, coupon redemption and account deletion under the same user lock. Cancellation, expiry, abandonment and failure leave all free months and allowances intact. After confirmed signup, Cancel renewal preserves every remaining free month and removes the future payment. Additional coupons extend this real subscription through the ordinary redemption flow; they never create an introductory trial or a fabricated redemption operation.

If setup finishes after the agreed date, Pumpkit first checks whether the authorized subscription already exists. If it does not, a fresh paid offer and acceptance are required. It never charges immediately off-session or substitutes a later date. A provider refusal of the exact date remains recoverable, with all benefits intact.

`POST /api/v1/billing/paid-signups/preview` reviews a plan; `POST /api/v1/billing/paid-signups` accepts its signed offer with a caller-generated operation identifier and fresh paid acceptance. `GET /api/v1/billing/paid-signups/{operation_id}` recovers its outcome; `POST /api/v1/billing/paid-signups/{operation_id}/cancel` cancels an unfinished arrangement. Recovery remains available when coupons are disabled. After free access expires, the Paywall offers the same signup status and cancellation actions before asking for fresh paid consent. Webhooks read current setup sessions, SetupIntent/payment-method ownership and every subscription, including ended history, before reconciling. Stable metadata and absolute targets survive Stripe's idempotency-key retention; a lost customer response is recovered through its user metadata before another customer can be created.

Run recovery periodically and alert on a nonzero exit or pending records. It prints operation identifiers and sanitized statuses, never payment details, codes or recipient addresses. Pending intent never ages out. Resolved signup recovery evidence is removed by the existing coupon purge 90 days after the later resolution and accepted free end; the accepted plan, price, interval, first-payment date and language remain with the existing paid Terms acceptance. Account deletion retains the existing paid-evidence lifecycle and cannot proceed with unresolved signup work.

### Paid-signup creation checkpoints

Migration `0050` records the selected payment method, the first creation checkpoint time, dispatch generation and cancellation intent. A mediator commits the checkpoint before sending the subscription request, then locks the user and reloads the operation. A stale dispatch owner cannot send after another generation takes over. Webhook subscription sync/deduplication commits first; paid-signup recovery then uses this same checkpoint orchestration. A repeated webhook can still recover pending signup work.

An uncertain response may mean the provider is still creating the subscription. An empty current subscription list cannot prove that request stopped. While the original consent and exact date remain valid, no cancellation was requested and fewer than 23 hours have passed since the original checkpoint, recovery can replay the identical provider key and parameters. The one-hour margin stays inside Stripe's documented minimum 24-hour idempotency retention. In-progress-key conflicts stay uncertain. The original checkpoint time never slides forward to renew this window. Recovery checks the original replay deadline again immediately before writing, after all provider reads and waits. A confirmed provider refusal is distinguished from an unknown request; a known failed checkpoint commit sends nothing.

Cancellation intent is committed before provider reads. After cancellation or expiry of an uncertain signup, recovery sends no new subscription creation, even inside that window. Billing changes, coupon redemption, account deletion and retention purge remain blocked until the request's outcome is proven. If the attributed subscription appears later, recovery cancels it and confirms its ended state before reporting cancellation complete. A provider-ended subscription is never reported as a confirmed renewal; an incomplete one remains pending. If delayed creation has already reached a positively settled paid interval, cancellation retains that proven paid coverage, its full monthly allowance and exact end through the existing paid-coverage fields. A free deferral or an unsettled invoice never becomes paid coverage, and existing payment debt remains owed. An unresolved request beyond the replay window remains pending for support/provider investigation, without any age-based release or inferred absence. A process crash before dispatch can therefore recover by bounded same-key replay while consent is valid; if cancellation or expiry intervenes before a proven outcome, support must establish that outcome rather than guessing. Free grants, accepted caps and anchors remain untouched throughout.

**Upgrade notes:** apply `0050` before serving this flow. Pending pre-checkpoint signup records are conservatively marked uncertain without inventing a first-attempt time, so they cannot automatically replay or be released merely because no subscription is visible. Inspect and resolve any such preactivation records with provider evidence before activation. Continue periodic recovery and alert on nonzero exits or unresolved rows. The existing fractional-timestamp limitation above remains unchanged. Cancellation samples the injected clock after provider waits and preserves coverage from the final authoritative subscription facts. Re-reading the same preserved paid interval keeps its captured caps even if configured limits change during cancellation. A stale browser can still show Cancel signup after another browser completed that signup and redeemed an additional coupon. If the completed commitment has changed, its original first-payment date has passed, or its cancellation cannot be confirmed, this old action returns `409 PAID_SIGNUP_CANCEL_CONFLICT` with current Settings guidance. It does not report signup completion as cancellation success or mutate an unrelated subscription. The browser retires those stale controls and refreshes the current plan card: use **Cancel renewal** for the actual remaining free period, or **Manage subscription** after it. Free and paid coverage and captured caps stay intact. Uncertain unfinished operations keep their recovery controls and guards; only this explicit completed-signup conflict retires stale recovery state. A legacy cancellation response saying `completed` is also displayed as an unconfirmed cancellation rather than "Subscription arranged".

Once cancellation validates the current attributed subscription, Pumpkit commits its pending cancellation state and any proven paid coverage before sending the provider mutation. Recovery reacquires the user lock and rechecks provider facts before dispatch. An ambiguous response therefore cannot roll back earned paid time or leave a completed record outside recovery: Settings status, webhooks and privileged recovery can finish it after the original first-payment date, including with coupons disabled. A lost final local commit retains that earlier checkpoint. Unpaid invoices never become paid coverage and still block account deletion. Changed or stale offers refused before this validated cancellation checkpoint keep the explicit current-plan conflict contract above.

## Deferral dispatch recovery and upgrade audit

Migration `0053` records a durable dispatch timestamp and generation before each provider deferral attempt. A process stop after sending, an in-progress key and a rejected retry all keep the original accepted target, claim reservation and paid/trial coverage. A definite first refusal or a confirmed no-write outcome in the current dispatch generation releases the claim without consuming the coupon. If an invoice, checkout, changed subscription or provider-read error prevents the first deferral from being sent, the operation is refused immediately; a later attempt needs a fresh preview. Once any earlier dispatch may have been sent, these same blockers keep the reservation pending and never reset its original replay deadline. Losing the local no-write refusal commit leaves the durable checkpoint uncertain, so recovery keeps its guards until the original operation is confirmed. Provider cancellation without the attributed target cannot settle an uncertain earlier write.

Recovery reuses the same key and exact absolute target for less than 23 hours from the first checkpoint, with a fresh clock check after provider reads. After that it only observes attributed provider evidence; it never releases a reservation based on age or an unchanged provider read. Repeating an absolute target does not add more months, but this implementation does not rely on writes beyond provider idempotency retention. GET, the recovery command and webhooks can still confirm a late attributed effect with coupons disabled. Investigate unresolved operations rather than guessing that they failed.

Before activation or upgrading an instance that tested earlier coupon implementations, apply `0053` and audit all pre-0053 pending and refused deferrals against provider request history, accepted dates and the original paid/trial coverage. Old attempt counters were committed after dispatch, so zero is not proof that no write was sent. The migration treats existing pending records as previously dispatched and reopens refused records as pending. A reopened refusal whose claim was deleted stays reserved and requires operator audit; automatic recovery cannot reconstruct its identity claim or erased original trial caps. Check for competing claims and restore verified reservation and coverage evidence before resolving it. Preserve the identity key ring and provider records; do not delete these operations or manufacture success/refusal from current old-state reads. Keep the feature off until that audit is complete. Instances that never enabled coupons have no such records.
